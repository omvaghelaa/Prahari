Prahari01 / 13 · Solution model & justification

The hybrid

It is not any one of the four reference models

Model 1 is the foundation the brief says it must be. Model 3 is the architecture built on top of it. From Models 2 and 4 we take a technique and an ambition — and reject Model 4's central video store outright.

ModelWhat we takeWhat we rejectWhere it lives

M2 Direct-attach viewing The direct RTSP / WHEP / HLS view — no transcode hop, no per-vendor console M2 as an architecture: it forbids the federation layer we depend on MediaMTX + the wall
M3 Federation middleware The structure — drivers behind one interface, one event bus, one API Its heavyweight per-site install gateway drivers, Redis Streams

One line for the jury: federation middleware with pluggable drivers — three drivers live, the departmental-VMS driver interface-complete pending vendor credentials.

the point   The grid permits exactly one of our four transports, and the platform found that out by itself — per camera, at startup, and recorded in camera:transport:<id>. Nothing downstream noticed.
Prahari04 / 13 · Video analytics — ANPR

The mandatory analytic

The pipeline, and the record it produces

Frame (PTS)decoded at 5 fps
Vehicle detectYOLOv8s · batch 16 · 20 ms flush
TrackByteTrack, one per camera
Plate detectYOLOv9-t ONNX, inside the vehicle crop
Four readersPaddleOCR · EasyOCR · fast-plate-ocr · Tesseract
Grammar + voteper-character (MVCP), refuses below 2/3

Plate detection runs on the vehicle crop, not the frame: a 20 px plate in a 1920 px frame is ~7 px after letterboxing; inside a 300 px crop it is a readable band. Grammar (^[A-Z]{2}[0-9]{2}[A-Z]{1,3}[0-9]{4}$, BH series too) is applied before the vote, so the vote spends itself only on genuine ambiguity.

Prahari06 / 13 · Analytics on real CCTV

The hard case, measured

A wide-area camera is not an ANPR camera. We measured how far short it falls.

Cameras on the Sentinel grid put a number plate into 20–30 px of frame. That is the regime the brief means by “challenging and varying CCTV conditions”, so it is the regime we benchmarked — rather than reporting a number from a clean plate and hoping.

The case the grid actually presents: a 20 px plate

Plate height, nightLR → OCRSingle-image SROurs — MFSR + MVCP
16 px0.0%0.0%5.0%
20 px0.0%0.0%65.0%
24 px0.0%15.0%95.0%

There is a cliff just under 20 px, and we admit it

The published reference for this regime — UFPR-SR-Plates, median plate height 18–21 px — reports 1.7–2.2% from a low-resolution crop straight to OCR, and 42–45% with super-resolution plus a per-character vote. That is the bar, and it is why a wide-area camera cannot be treated as an ANPR camera.

A plate face is about 65% glyph, so 20 px of plate is ~13 px of glyph — the bottom of what CRNN and SVTR recognisers were trained on. Below ~10 px the pipeline refuses, and the wall says so on the tile.

why this matters to a procurement   It separates a siting problem from a model problem. A plate under ~20 px will not read on any software; the answer is a camera at the right place, and the platform saying so on the tile instead of printing noise.
Prahari08 / 13 · Watchlist correlation & alerts

Real-time alerts

Banded matching, an audited state machine

Matching is banded, not binary: exact plate_norm, then plate_canon (confusion classes collapsed), then weighted edit distance ≤ 1.0. Watchlist categories: stolen vehicles, wanted / missing persons, blacklisted vehicles, suspect lists.

NEW→ ACKNOWLEDGED→ ACTIONED | DISMISSED (reason required)

Every transition is written to the audit log with a user and a reason. Repeated sightings of one vehicle bump a count on the open alert — twenty alerts for one event is how an operator learns to ignore them.

measured   Pipeline half — frame in to sighting published: 0.68 s against a 3.0 s budget (services/worker/selftest.py). Dedup holds one alert per (entry, camera) for 60 s; an illegal transition is HTTP 409 and writes nothing.
Prahari09 / 13 · The graded test case

Route reconstruction

A registration number in. A defensible route out.

A judge types a vehicle registration; the map draws that vehicle's hops across the grid with timestamps and locations, snapped to roads via OSRM.

What it returns

  • Ordered hops: camera, coordinate, timestamp
  • Implied speed on each leg
  • CSV export, audited

What it refuses

  • A hop needing 180 km/h through a city is flagged IMPLAUSIBLE, not quietly drawn
  • A route that cannot lie to an officer is worth more than one that always answers

Expected output for the jury: complete route, timestamped and location-wise, plus the live watchlist cross-referencing and auto-alerts running alongside it.

Prahari10 / 13 · Security & accountability

RBAC + audit

Enforced in SQL. Hiding a button is not access control.

The department-scope matrix is enforced in the database with row-level security as the backstop — not in the UI. A district viewer's query simply returns fewer rows; a system administrator may not see live data at all.

Every action is logged

  • Every export and every live view writes an audit row
  • Cross-department access needs a case number, a 72 h cap and target-department approval
  • The log is hash-chained; a tamper breaks the chain

Integration-ready

  • APIs shaped for VAHAN, SARTHI, eGujCop (CCTNS), AFIS, NAFIS
  • Documented, open protocols — no lock-in on cameras, VMS, analytics or storage
verified   GET /api/admin/audit/verify → 728 rows, ok: true, first_broken_seq: null. And the row the matrix exists for: SYSTEM_ADMIN is refused live data, alerts and route — the most privileged account in the system cannot watch anybody.
Prahari11 / 13 · Scalability & PoC readiness

The arithmetic, printed so the jury can check it

Four orders of magnitude, in four multiplications

Centralised video — the M4 approach we reject:

80,000 cameras × 2 Mbps (H.264, 720p, 5 fps) = 160,000 Mbps ≈ 160 Gbps 160 Gbps × 86,400 s/day ≈ 1.7 PB/day ≈ 26 PB at ~15-day retention

Metadata — what we actually ship:

~200 bytes per sighting row ([C1]) 80,000 cameras × ~1,000 sightings/camera/day ≈ 80 M rows/day 80 M × 200 B ≈ 16 GB/day raw ≈ 2–3 GB/day compressed (Timescale)

26 PB → 16 GB/day. That is the entire case for edge inference.

Scale plan also covers: central / regional / edge compute, GPU sizing, low-bandwidth operation, hot / warm / cold storage by retention, HA, backup, DR, cybersecurity, and a capex + opex estimate.
